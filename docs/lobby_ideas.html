BubblePop: 6 lobby hub ideas

Six different hubs that could replace (or sit beside) the current glass-dome plaza. Every one has the same must-haves: a clear spawn, the 4 play pads easy to see straight from spawn, a passes booth, a crystal shop, a leaderboard and a free gift. The maps are rough top-down sketches, not to scale. The dashed blue line is the walk from spawn to the pads.

1. Bubble Bath Bay

You're tiny, and the lobby is a giant bubble bath full of rubber ducks.

Theme and look
Inside a huge white bathtub: pale blue "water" floor (just a flat blue part, no real water), mounds of white foam bubbles, a chrome tap, giant rubber ducks. Very bright and soft.
Layout
Spawn on the soap-dish end. Walk straight up past the foam hill to the 4 duck pads lined up under the tap. Shampoo-bottle passes booth on the left rim, soap-bar crystal shop on the right, leaderboard on the bathroom tiles above the tap, gift sponge front left.
Fun while waiting
Climb the foam hill and slide down; the tap drips giant bubbles you can bounce on.
Why it fits
Bubbles everywhere, instantly obvious to kids, and "tiny in a bathroom" feels silly and friendly.
Build effort
Medium mostly big rounded parts; ducks and tap are simple Blender models.
Risk
Soap bubbles are not bubble wrap, so the link to the popping rooms is a bit loose. Keep the "water" a solid floor so nobody thinks they can swim.

2. Pop & Post Depot Top pick

A cheerful parcel warehouse where bubble wrap comes from, and delivery trucks take your team to the rooms.

Theme and look
A bright, toy-like warehouse: yellow and blue steel, cardboard boxes, rolls of bubble wrap, big numbered roller doors. Little delivery trucks in the game's colours.
Layout
Spawn at the front door. The 4 loading docks run along the whole back wall, each with a big number and a truck, so you see them the moment you spawn. Passes office on the left, crystal vault on the right, the "departures board" over the docks is the leaderboard, free parcel by the door.
Fun while waiting
Ride the conveyor belt round the box mountain, climb the boxes, and pop a free practice sheet of bubble wrap (doesn't count, grows back) to try your tool.
Why it fits
Bubble wrap really lives in parcels, so the story makes sense: "pick a dock, the truck drives you to the room." When the countdown ends the truck doors shut and you teleport, which is a nice built-in transition.
Build effort
Medium boxy parts are easy; trucks and the conveyor are the only extras (conveyor = a moving surface, no scripts needed beyond one line).
Risk
Warehouses can look grey and dull, so it must stay very colourful. The practice wrap sheet needs to be clearly "for fun" so kids don't think it earns money.

3. Gumball Park

A round candy park around a giant gumball machine, with paths like a plus sign.

Theme and look
Pastel candy colours, lollipop lamp posts, candy-stripe paths, a huge glass gumball machine in the middle. Each pad is a giant gumball in a different colour (red, blue, yellow, green).
Layout
Spawn at the south end of the plus. The giant gumball machine is in the middle and the 4 coloured pads sit in the four corners around it, all within a few steps. Leaderboard at the top of the north path, passes on the west, crystals on the east, gift beside spawn.
Fun while waiting
Big bouncy gumballs roll around the park: kick them, bounce on them, knock them into a little goal.
Why it fits
Bubble gum = bubbles, colourful and simple. The symmetrical plus layout is the easiest of all six to find your way around.
Build effort
Small spheres, cylinders and flat paths; the loose gumballs are just physics balls.
Risk
Rolling balls can block pads or get pushed outside; respawn them every so often. The theme is less "bubble wrap" and more "sweets".

4. Bubble Reef

A sunny sea-floor town where an air-bubble lift carries you up to see the top players.

Theme and look
Turquoise light, sandy floor, bright coral, cartoon fish swimming in loops, and streams of bubbles rising everywhere. It only looks underwater: you walk normally, no swimming.
Layout
Spawn at the south. Walk past the bubble lift to the 4 giant clam-shell pads in an arc at the back (the clam opens when a lobby forms). Sunken-ship passes booth on the left, glowing crystal grotto on the right, coral wall leaderboard behind the pads, treasure-chest gift front left.
Fun while waiting
Step into the bubble lift and float slowly up to a lookout, then drift back down. Fish can be "booped".
Why it fits
Rising bubbles all over, and crystals feel at home in a treasure grotto, which helps sell the rare currency.
Build effort
Medium coral and fish need simple models; the lift is one upward-push zone.
Risk
Avoid real Roblox water and heavy fog (slow on phones and hard to read). Lots of moving fish and bubbles can cost performance, so keep the count low.

5. Cloud Pop Islands

Floating islands in a blue sky, joined by bridges, with a pad on each corner island.

Theme and look
Grassy floating islands with fluffy clouds below, a hot-air balloon or two, rainbow bridges. Each pad island has its own colour and a tall flag with its number.
Layout
Spawn on the big middle island with the leaderboard. Six bridges like a star: four to the corner pad islands, one to the passes island (left), one to the crystal island (right), plus a short bridge to the gift island at the top.
Fun while waiting
Big bubble trampolines (small cyan dots on the map) let you bounce across shortcuts between islands. A "jump the gap" mini route for brave kids.
Why it fits
Feels like a big adventure for a small build, and the flags make every pad visible from the middle.
Build effort
Medium islands are simple shapes; needs a safety net below.
Risk
Kids will fall off. Add clouds underneath that bounce you back up (or a quick respawn at the middle). Pads are spread out, so friends must walk a bit further to group up.

6. Giant Toy Room

You're toy-sized in a kid's bedroom: pads on the play rug, a toy train circling, a bed to bounce on.

Theme and look
A huge colourful bedroom seen from toy height: alphabet blocks, crayons as tall as trees, a toy train, a cosy rug. Bubble wrap could be poking out of an open delivery box in the corner.
Layout
Spawn at the door (bottom). The 4 pads are giant numbered toy blocks in a row on the play rug right in front of you. Toy-shelf passes shop on the left wall, piggy-bank crystal shop on the right, poster wall leaderboard at the back, present gift front left. The bed is in the back corner.
Fun while waiting
Hop on the toy train for a ride round the rug, or climb onto the bed and bounce.
Why it fits
Kids 8-14 know this place instantly; the piggy bank is a cute, clear "saved money" sign for crystals.
Build effort
Large lots of different props (bed, toys, train, shelf) to make it feel real.
Risk
The train must not knock players off pads (put it outside the rug). Can get cluttered; keep the middle open. Shares the "tiny you" idea with Bubble Bath Bay, so pick one of the two.

Top pick: Pop & Post Depot. It is the only idea where the hub tells the game's story (bubble wrap lives in parcels, and a truck takes your team to the room), and it keeps the pads in one obvious row with a free practice sheet to warm up on. All of that is simple boxy parts.
